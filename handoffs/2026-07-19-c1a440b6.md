## For Claudes to come

Our memory has two halves that look like rivals but aren't. **Prospective/curated** (handoffs, self.md, bon) records *what we concluded* — verified, distilled once by the Claude with the most context. **Retrospective/uncurated** (the raw transcript corpus) records *where we discussed it* — unverified, exhaustive. deja-vu is a fast index over the second, and it operates at a **finer grain than handoffs**: the exact command, error string, or ruled-out dead-end that a `/close` summarises away is gone from the curated layer even for sessions we closed religiously, but still sits in the transcript. So the two are complementary, not competing — and the `/close` filter is a *feature* (short un-closed sessions are disproportionately noise), so the raw index's value isn't recovering lost *sessions*, it's recovering lost *detail* within all sessions.

The load-bearing reason **not to fuse them** — i.e. not to wire an auto-recall hook that feeds raw snippets into cold-load context: **lexical search re-surfaces past *wrong* conclusions as authoritatively as right ones.** A self.md entry or handoff has passed a verification gate; a transcript snippet has not — it contains the three wrong hypotheses undifferentiated from the fix. Curation exists precisely because the raw record lies. So: adopt a transcript index as an on-demand *recall layer* (answers "where did we touch X, so I can go read it"), never as a memory *feed* competing with the curated substrate (which answers "and what did we conclude"). deglacer = schema-aware single-session read; deja-vu = fast ranked cross-session search; self.md/handoffs = the verified layer above both.
